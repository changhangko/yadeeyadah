SPACE / SELECTED ARCHITECTURE

60 Denmark Street

A residential masterplan shaped through massing, solar access, public realm and street-scale decisions — presented as the primary architectural case study, followed by two shorter professional studies.

KEW / MELBOURNEBATES SMART / LENDLEASE2025
01
MASSING + SOLAR

Density was treated as an environmental problem, not only a yield problem.

The building forms were organised as slender north–south volumes to protect solar access to dwellings and landscape. Grasshopper/Ladybug studies were used alongside physical and digital massing to test the consequences of orientation before the scheme was resolved.

60%+of façades positioned to capture at least two hours of winter-solstice sunlight.
90%of residences described in the submission as receiving direct solar access in winter.
5h+direct solar access reported for the primary public open space.
02
PUBLIC REALM + STREETWALL

The taller forms are mediated at the scale of the street.

A large north-facing public open space anchors the precinct, while three-to-five-storey street walls reduce the perceived scale of the taller buildings and create a more continuous pedestrian edge.

SELECTED PROFESSIONAL WORK

03 / IGLU ARDEN

Façade expression as a system of proportion, depth and identity.

The feasibility and façade study tests podium and tower expression across two related but distinct identities. Repeated frames, planting, setbacks and vertical rhythms create variation without losing a coherent family resemblance.

04 / 87 QUEENSBRIDGE / OSK

Tower form as a negotiation between views, shadow, wind and identity.

The competition study develops a triangular tower logic: the geometry opens oblique views, reduces shadow and wind impacts relative to a square alternative, and creates three clear perimeter zones around the core.

01
THE PROBLEM

Useful resources existed, but finding them required knowing the system first.

The issue was not a lack of information. Resources were split across software names, individual pages and document libraries, so a simple design question could turn into a search through several unrelated entry points.

01 / FIND

Too many entry points

The same task could begin from a software page, a document library or an internal link.

02 / NAME

You had to know the name first

Searching worked best when you already knew the tool or resource you were looking for.

03 / CONTEXT

Tools were separated from design tasks

Resources were organised by system ownership rather than by what a designer was trying to do.

WEB DESIGN LOGIC

The visual system follows the same idea as the information architecture: fewer entry points, visible states and short paths between a question and a usable resource. The blue grid establishes one recognisable environment; repeated components make different libraries feel related without forcing their content into the same layout.

01 / CITY MODELS

Find a city model by place

Start from location and available coverage instead of searching through model filenames.

02 / MATERIALS

Browse materials visually

03 / FAÇADE

Bring guidance into the task

Surface relevant façade references where the design question appears, rather than in a separate knowledge silo.

04 / COLOUR

Compare colour references together

Keep related colour information in one place so alternatives can be compared quickly.
